Macy's Parade Hits 100: Where to Stand, Who's Singing

The centennial march steps off at 8:30 a.m. Thursday on Central Park West, with new balloons, DEVO, Smash Mouth and Queen Latifah.

Thursday morning the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade marches for the 100th time, and the city is acting like it. The first one ran in 1924; the count lands on 100 this year because the parade sat out three years during World War II, as Travel Pirates notes. It steps off at 8:30 a.m. rain or shine, per FOX 5 NY, and wraps in front of Macy's Herald Square around noon.

The route

The parade runs about 2.5 miles: it starts at West 77th Street and Central Park West, rolls down to Columbus Circle, east on 59th Street, straight down Sixth Avenue and west on 34th Street to Herald Square, according to FOX 5 NY. Rockefeller Center's guide says the lead balloons usually reach Sixth Avenue at 59th Street around 9:30 a.m., so the start time is not when it reaches you.

Where to stand

Macy's says public viewing is usually open along Sixth Avenue between 59th and 38th Streets and on Central Park West between 75th and 61st Streets, and it suggests showing up around 6 a.m., FOX 5 NY reports. Skip the starting line at 77th Street, Columbus Circle, the TV zone between 38th and 34th Streets and the blocks around Herald Square: those are closed to the public. Some streets close Wednesday, and closures run at the NYPD's discretion. Watching from the sidewalk costs nothing and needs no ticket.

What's new in the sky

FOX 5 NY lists eight new balloons: Bluey, Hello Kitty, Joe Cool and Woodstock, Sorcerer's Apprentice Mickey, Buckaroo Bob the Minion, Billie Brightpaw, Earl the Squirrel, and Underdog, back for the first time since 1984, according to Rockefeller Center. New floats include a Harry Potter Hogwarts Holiday float and Tony Sarg's Amazing Marionettes.

Who's on the floats

The lineup runs from Queen Latifah, Leslie Odom Jr., Keke Palmer and Anthony Ramos to DEVO, Smash Mouth, Blues Traveler, Natalie Merchant, Teddy Swims, Wyclef Jean, Megan Moroney and K-pop group RIIZE, per FOX 5 NY. Santa closes the show, as always.

Staying in

NBC and Peacock carry it live from 8:30 a.m., with Savannah Guthrie, Hoda Kotb and Al Roker hosting. Dress in layers either way: late-November mornings on the route run cold and windy.
