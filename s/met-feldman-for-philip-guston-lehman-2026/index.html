Morton Feldman's For Philip Guston Fills the Met's Lehman Wing

Friday, November 13 from 3 to 7:30 PM in the Robert Lehman Wing at the Met on Fifth Avenue in the Upper East Side.

On Friday afternoon, November 13, the Robert Lehman Wing at the Metropolitan Museum of Art becomes a concert space for one of the longest works in American music. Flutist Alice Teyssier, percussionist Nathan Davis and pianist Manuel Laufer, who also plays celesta, present Morton Feldman's For Philip Guston, a piece that runs nearly five hours.

The music

Feldman wrote For Philip Guston in the 1980s as a tribute to the painter, whom he knew and admired. The music moves at a deliberate pace, and the program is built to be heard slowly, so the length is part of the experience rather than an obstacle to it.

Plan your visit

The performance runs from 3 to 7:30 PM, and listeners may come and go at any point. Admission is included with museum admission. The Lehman Wing is at 1000 Fifth Avenue at 82nd Street in the Upper East Side, a short walk from the 86th Street stops on the 4, 5 and 6 trains.
