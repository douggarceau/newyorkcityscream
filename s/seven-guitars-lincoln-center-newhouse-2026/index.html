Seven Guitars Begins Previews at Lincoln Center Theater Tonight

Previews begin Thursday, November 5 at the Mitzi E. Newhouse Theater, with opening night on Monday, November 23.

Lincoln Center Theater opens its new season with a revival of August Wilson's Seven Guitars, a play first staged in the mid-1990s and set in 1948 Pittsburgh. It is one of the ten plays in Wilson's cycle about African American life in the city, with each play set in a different decade of the twentieth century.

The play opens with the funeral of a blues guitarist, Floyd "Schoolboy" Barton, and then moves back through the days before his death, following the people who gathered around him in a Pittsburgh backyard.

The company

Ruben Santiago-Hudson directs a cast led by Russell Hornsby as Floyd "Schoolboy" Barton, with Chanté Adams as Ruby, Michael Potts as Red Carter, Kristolyn Lloyd as Vera, Jason Dirden as Canewell, Roslyn Ruff as Louise and John Douglas Thompson as Hedley. Michael Carnahan designs the scenery, Karen Perry the costumes, Rui Rita the lighting and Justin Ellington the sound. Junior Mack wrote the original music, and Kenneth L. Roberson choreographed the movement.

When and where

The production plays the Mitzi E. Newhouse Theater, 150 West 65th Street, inside Lincoln Center. Previews begin Thursday, November 5, and opening night is Monday, November 23. The run is listed through early January 2027. Tickets are sold through Lincoln Center Theater, which is the place to check performance times and seat availability.
