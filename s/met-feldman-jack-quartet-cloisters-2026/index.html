JACK Quartet Plays Morton Feldman at The Met Cloisters on November 14

Saturday, November 14 at 2 PM in the Fuentidueña Chapel at The Met Cloisters, with the JACK Quartet and violinist Keir GoGwilt.

The Met's Celebrating Morton Feldman series brings the American composer's music to two of the museum's spaces later this week. On Friday, November 13 at 3 PM, a performance of For Philip Guston takes place in the Robert Lehman Wing on Fifth Avenue.

The highlight for many listeners is Saturday, November 14 at 2 PM, when the JACK Quartet and violinist Keir GoGwilt perform Feldman's Violin and String Quartet in the Fuentidueña Chapel at The Met Cloisters. The chapel, built around a 12th-century Spanish apse, offers an intimate setting for music that rewards slow, close listening.

Who was Feldman

Feldman, who lived from 1926 to 1987, wrote quiet, slow music in which sounds hang in the air and patterns shift almost imperceptibly. He was close to the painters of the New York School, and his friendship with the painter Philip Guston led to For Philip Guston, one of his best-known works.

Some of Feldman's late works run for several hours, and his chamber music asks listeners to settle into long, unhurried time. The Cloisters setting, among the museum's medieval galleries and gardens, suits that stillness well.

Getting there

The Cloisters is at 99 Margaret Corbin Drive in Fort Tryon Park, in Upper Manhattan. The Robert Lehman Wing is in the main museum at 1000 Fifth Avenue. Check the Met's website for ticketing and admission details before you go.
