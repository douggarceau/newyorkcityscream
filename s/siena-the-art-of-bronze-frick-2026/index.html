Siena: The Art of Bronze Fills the Frick Through January 18

Nearly forty Sienese bronzes, including works by Donatello, are on view at the Frick Collection, 1 East 70th Street, through January 18, 2027.

The Frick Collection has opened Siena: The Art of Bronze, 1450 to 1500, a show that asks New Yorkers to look again at a Renaissance city better known for painting. The exhibition brings together nearly forty bronzes, including statues, reliefs, candelabra and medals, and it is the first survey to examine Siena as a center of bronze sculpture innovation.

The artists

The show's key figures are Donatello, Vecchietta and Francesco di Giorgio. Many of the works are rarely seen outside Tuscany, which makes the Lauder galleries an unusual chance to study them side by side. The exhibition also explains the technical challenges Sienese bronzeworkers faced.

The curator and catalogue

Giulio Dalvit, the Frick's Associate Curator, organized the exhibition. He is writing the accompanying catalogue with international contributors, so the show is also a lasting record of the research behind it.

Visiting

Siena: The Art of Bronze is in the Ronald S. Lauder Exhibition Galleries at 1 East 70th Street, New York, NY 10021, and runs from October 15, 2026, through January 18, 2027. The Frick recommends timed tickets. Frick members visit at no charge, and ticket prices for other visitors are not listed on the museum's exhibition page.
