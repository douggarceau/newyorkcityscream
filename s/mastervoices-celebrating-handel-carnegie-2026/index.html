MasterVoices Celebrates Handel at Carnegie Hall on December 15

Tuesday, Dec. 15 at 7:30 p.m. in Carnegie Hall's Stern Auditorium in Midtown, Ted Sperling leads the MasterVoices Chorus and New York Baroque in a Handel holiday celebration.

Winter's first big choral night in Midtown arrives on Tuesday, Dec. 15, when the MasterVoices Chorus and New York Baroque Incorporated bring Handel's music to Carnegie Hall at 7:30 p.m. Ted Sperling conducts the holiday-season concert, which features soprano Erin Morley and countertenor Jakub Józef Orliński. The chorus of 120 singers is joined by 29 period-instrument players.

An evening of Handel's heroes and gods

The program draws on several of Handel's most dramatic works, including Semele, Giulio Cesare, Israel in Egypt and Agrippina. The stories it tells range from heroic to divine to romantic, and the music moves between big choral numbers and intimate solo arias that give the two soloists room to shine.

A singalong to finish

The evening closes with an audience singalong of the Hallelujah Chorus, the famous moment from Handel's oratorio Messiah. Everyone in the hall is invited to stand and join in, which makes the final minutes a warm way to start the holiday season.

Reception and tickets

A benefit reception follows in Carnegie Hall's Rohatyn Room and Rose Museum. Concert tickets are priced from $29 including fees, and reception tickets start at $500. Tickets are available through the Carnegie Hall website, by calling 212-247-7800, or at the box office, which is open Monday through Saturday from 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. and Sunday from noon to 6 p.m. Hours may change at the hall's discretion.

Handel's long London career

Handel built much of his career in London, where his operas and oratorios were performed for audiences across the 18th century. Israel in Egypt is one of his oratorios, and Semele and Giulio Cesare come from the operatic stage, so the program shows how the same composer moved between the theatre and the concert hall.
