Lincoln in the Bardo Closes Its Metropolitan Opera Run on November 14

Missy Mazzoli's supernatural opera, based on George Saunders' novel, plays its last Met performances through Saturday, November 14.

The Metropolitan Opera's Lincoln in the Bardo plays its final performances on November 14. The run was scheduled from October 19 at the Met Opera House at Lincoln Center, 30 Lincoln Center Plaza. The opera, with music by Missy Mazzoli and a libretto by Royce Vavrek, was announced as a world premiere in the company's 2026–27 season.

It is based on George Saunders' Booker Prize-winning novel, a supernatural story set in the bardo, the in-between place Buddhist tradition describes between death and what follows. The novel centers on Abraham Lincoln's grief for his young son Willie, and the opera keeps that emotional core while moving among the spirits who gather in a cemetery.

The novel won the Booker Prize in 2017, and its unusual structure, built from a chorus of voices in the cemetery, made it a natural subject for the stage.

The team and the cast

Lileana Blain-Cruz directs the production, and Yannick Nézet-Séguin conducts. Peter Mattei sings the title role of Lincoln, and Christine Goerke takes the part of the Reverend. Anthony Roth Costanzo sings Miss Elise Traynor, while Jonah Hoskins and Ryan McKinney take the roles of Bevins and Vollman.

Tickets and plans

Tickets are sold through the Met's own box office and website. Check the Met's calendar for the exact time of each performance and for seat options before you go, and allow time to reach Lincoln Center, which is served by the 1 train at 66th Street and the A, B, C and D trains at 59th Street–Columbus Circle.
