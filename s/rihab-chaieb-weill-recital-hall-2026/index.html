Mezzo-Soprano Rihab Chaieb Sings Songs From Arabic Poetry at Carnegie

Wednesday, November 18 at 7:30 PM in Weill Recital Hall at Carnegie Hall, with pianist Ammiel Bushakevitz.

Mezzo-soprano Rihab Chaieb and pianist Ammiel Bushakevitz bring a recital to Weill Recital Hall at Carnegie Hall on Wednesday, November 18 at 7:30 PM. The hall sits inside the Carnegie Hall complex at 881 Seventh Avenue, at West 57th Street, and its smaller room suits a close, personal kind of listening.

The program moves from French art song to songs rooted in Arabic poetry and music. It opens with Saint-Saëns' Mélodies persanes, then turns to Aubert's Six poèmes arabes and Saint-Saëns' Désir de l'orient. Selections from Osvaldo Golijov's Ayre follow, along with a traditional song, Lamma Bada Yatathanna, and works by Jouini, Fairuz and Al-Ghozzi.

The mix of languages gives the evening a wide range of color, moving from the French lines of the opening songs to the Arabic texts that follow.

Fairuz's Li Beirut is built on the Adagio from Joaquín Rodrigo's Concierto de Aranjuez, which makes the second half a lovely blend of two traditions. The recital also includes the US premiere of Matthew Ricketts's Nomad[e].
