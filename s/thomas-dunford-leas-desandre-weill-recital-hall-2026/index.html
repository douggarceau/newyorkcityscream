Thomas Dunford and Léa Desandre: Baroque Arias at Weill Recital Hall

Tuesday, November 17, doors 7:30 PM, Weill Recital Hall at Carnegie Hall, Midtown.

Tuesday, November 17 brings a rare chance to hear the lutenist Thomas Dunford and the French mezzo-soprano Léa Desandre in an intimate setting. Their concert is presented in Weill Recital Hall, the smaller of Carnegie Hall's halls, at 57th Street and Seventh Avenue in Midtown. Doors open at 7:30 PM. The listing does not give a start time, so check Carnegie Hall's calendar before you head out.

A program of Baroque song

Dunford leads the Jupiter Ensemble, an early music group, and the evening draws largely on the ensemble's Amazone program. That program gathers French, Italian and German Baroque arias, a repertoire built on the close bond between voice and instrument. Dunford's lute and Desandre's singing form the core of the evening, and a recital hall suits music written to be heard at close range.

Early music has a devoted following in New York, and Carnegie's recital series offers a chance to hear it without the distance of a large auditorium. Each aria is a self-contained portrait of longing, grief or elegant wit, so the audience can follow the story from one song to the next. Arriving a little early gives time to settle in before the first note.

Tickets and getting there

The listing shows tickets on sale, but it gives no prices. Weill Recital Hall is inside the Carnegie Hall complex at 881 Seventh Avenue, New York, NY 10019. Carnegie Hall's 57th Street and Seventh Avenue entrance is a short walk from several subway lines, and the hall is easy to reach on foot from Midtown offices and hotels.
