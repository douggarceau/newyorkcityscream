Architects of Liberation at MoMA: Modernism in Western Africa

On view on the third floor of MoMA's Robert B. Menschel Galleries through January 2, 2027, with about 400 objects from seven countries.

Architects of Liberation: Modernism in Western Africa is the first major museum exhibition to examine how architecture figured in the years of decolonization in West Africa. It is on view on the third floor of the Robert B. Menschel Galleries at MoMA, and the museum's listing gives January 2, 2027 as the closing date.

What is on view

The show brings together roughly 400 objects from seven countries. Many have never been shown publicly. They include drawings, architectural models, archival images, and newly commissioned photographs and videos that together trace how newly independent nations used modern buildings to present a forward-looking image of themselves to the world.

The curators

The exhibition was organized by Martino Stierli, the Philip Johnson Chief Curator of Architecture and Design at MoMA, with guest curator Ikem Stanley Okoye, an associate professor at the University of Delaware, and Mallory Cohen, a curatorial associate in the museum's architecture and design department.

Visiting

The museum's listing gives the venue and the closing date but no admission price or opening date, so check moma.org for current admission and timed-entry details before you go. MoMA is at 11 West 53rd Street in Midtown.

Why it matters

Architecture from this period is seen less often in New York, and this show puts the buildings themselves at the center. It suits visitors who enjoy design history and want a museum visit that reaches beyond the usual painting and sculpture galleries.
